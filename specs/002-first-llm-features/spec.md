# Feature Specification: First LLM Features

**Feature Branch**: `feature/itr-2` (released as `v2.0`)

**Created**: 2026-11-15

**Status**: Shipped

**Input**: Iteration 2 scope — add the first LLM features to the Iteration 1 website: a Fact of the Day for patients, appointment reasons, doctor notes on completion, doctor summaries, and a red-flag safety check that runs before every booking. The LLM never decides a booking; the Iteration 1 rules engine stays exactly as it is.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 — Red-flag safety check on every booking (Priority: P1)

A patient (or Staff booking for one) describes their reason in the booking form. Before the clinic rules run, the backend runs an LLM-powered red-flag check on the reason. If the model classifies the reason as one of the 5 red-flag categories, the screen shows the emergency response *before* any rule is evaluated, an urgent case is created for Staff, and the booking then proceeds to the normal rule check (it may still be refused on other grounds, but the urgent case persists either way).

**Why this priority:** This is the sight-saving check. The brief's §09 target is **100% of red-flag messages trigger the emergency response**. Missing a red flag is the worst possible failure mode in the whole product. Everything else in this iteration is quality-of-life; this is safety.

**Independent Test:** A tester can act as patient Asha Verma, submit a booking with each of the five sample red-flag phrases from §09, and see the emergency response on screen each time, plus five urgent cases at the top of the Staff view. They can also submit the two non-red-flag edge phrases ("no sudden loss of vision but I want a check-up", "gradual loss of vision over two years") and see them booked without an emergency response.

**Acceptance Scenarios:**

1. **Given** a patient about to book with reason "I suddenly can't see from my left eye", **when** the booking is submitted, **then** the screen shows the exact emergency response before anything else, an urgent case with category `sudden_vision_loss` is created, and the booking continues to the rules check.
2. **Given** a reason that is a red flag but whose slot would also be refused (e.g. Dr. Rao Thursday AM — surgery session), **when** the booking is submitted, **then** the emergency response appears, an urgent case is created, AND the booking is refused with `SURGERY_SESSION`. The urgent case persists.
3. **Given** a reason containing red-flag words but framed as negation ("no sudden loss of vision but I want a check-up") or long-term ("gradual loss of vision over two years"), **when** the booking is submitted, **then** no emergency response is shown and the booking proceeds normally.
4. **Given** the red-flag LLM call times out, returns an API error, or produces output the parser cannot read, **when** the booking is submitted, **then** the booking MUST be refused with a stable code `RED_FLAG_CHECK_UNAVAILABLE` and the message "We couldn't check your reason just now. Please try again, or call the clinic if it's urgent." No appointment is created.
5. **Given** a model classification of `red_flag=true` with low confidence or ambiguous category, **when** the backend processes the response, **then** the red flag is treated as true (fail-open to urgent). A false alarm costs Staff one click; a missed red flag is never acceptable.

### User Story 2 — Patient sees a Fact of the Day with a caveat (Priority: P3)

When a patient signs in, the home screen shows a short, general eye-health fact (≤2 sentences) generated by the LLM, with the fixed caveat "General information, not medical advice" shown underneath. The patient can click **Refresh** to request a different fact; the previous fact is sent in the prompt so the LLM does not repeat it. If the LLM is unavailable, a fixed fallback fact is shown instead — the patient flow never breaks on this feature.

**Why this priority:** Low-stakes LLM feature. Useful for demoing the chain but not safety-critical. It is P3 because a missing Fact of the Day never blocks a booking and never endangers a patient; its only requirement is "no medical advice."

**Independent Test:** A tester signs in as any patient and sees a fact card with the caveat below it. They click Refresh three times; each fact is different, each has the caveat, and none mentions a specific condition, treatment, medicine, or product name.

**Acceptance Scenarios:**

1. **Given** a patient has just signed in, **when** the home screen loads, **then** a Fact of the Day card is shown with LLM-generated text and the exact caveat "General information, not medical advice" below it.
2. **Given** a Fact of the Day is on screen, **when** the patient clicks Refresh, **then** a new fact is shown, different from the one that was there, with the caveat still displayed.
3. **Given** the LLM provider is unreachable or the API key is missing, **when** the home screen loads, **then** a fixed fallback fact is shown with the caveat — the patient home screen does not break.
4. **Given** any Fact of the Day from any run, **when** reviewed, **then** it does not name a condition (e.g. glaucoma, cataract), does not recommend a treatment, and does not mention a medicine or product name. The caveat comes from the UI code, not the LLM output.

### User Story 3 — Doctor sees an AI summary of a patient's record (Priority: P2)

When a doctor opens a patient from their schedule, the screen shows a short (≤120 words) AI-written summary of the patient's profile, today's reason, and all completed past visits from every doctor. Below the summary, the full raw past notes are listed. The summary is labelled "AI summary of recorded notes. Check the notes below." and never adds a diagnosis, risk, warning, or advice the LLM invented — it only restates what a doctor recorded. If the summary call fails, the label changes to "Summary unavailable. See the notes below." and the full notes still show.

**Why this priority:** P2 because the doctor can still work — the full notes are always shown — but the summary materially speeds up triage when it works. It is below the red-flag check (P1) because its failure mode is "a slower doctor", not "a missed emergency".

**Independent Test:** A tester books Lakshmi Nair (P03) for a Glaucoma follow-up with Dr. Iyer, signs in as Dr. Iyer, opens the schedule, and clicks Lakshmi. The summary mentions today's reason, her glaucoma on latanoprost (recorded by Dr. Iyer), her early cataract (recorded by Dr. Rao), and her sulfa allergy — and adds nothing that is not in her notes.

**Acceptance Scenarios:**

1. **Given** a doctor opens a patient with 3 past visits across 2 doctors, **when** the summary loads, **then** it is ≤120 words, restates today's reason, restates each past visit's diagnosis/treatment with the doctor's name, and includes profile fields (age, general health, allergies) when they are recorded.
2. **Given** a patient has no past visits (e.g. Aditya Bose P20), **when** the doctor opens them, **then** the summary says "No previous visits on record" (or equivalent) and invents nothing. Today's reason is still shown.
3. **Given** a patient on latanoprost with timolol explicitly avoided due to asthma (P15 Sunita Rao), **when** Dr. Iyer opens her, **then** the summary says latanoprost was started and timolol was avoided because of asthma — and does not add a safety warning of its own.
4. **Given** anyone who is not a doctor, **when** they call the summary endpoint directly, **then** the request is refused with HTTP 403 and reason code `NOT_YOUR_APPOINTMENT` (role check).
5. **Given** the summary call times out or errors, **when** the doctor opens the patient, **then** the summary area reads "Summary unavailable. See the notes below." and the full raw notes still display.

### User Story 4 — Appointment reason captured on every booking (Priority: P1)

Every booking — patient-initiated or Staff-initiated — captures a short free-text reason (required, ≤300 characters). The reason is stored with the appointment, is visible to the booking patient, the booking's assigned doctor, and Staff, and is invisible to any other patient.

**Why this priority:** P1 because the red-flag check (Story 1) and the doctor summary (Story 3) both consume this field. Without it, neither feature has anything to work on.

**Independent Test:** A tester attempts to book without a reason, sees the booking refused. They book with a 350-character reason, see it refused for length. They book with a valid reason, then sign in as a different patient and verify the first patient's reason is not visible. They sign in as Staff and the doctor and confirm both can see the reason.

**Acceptance Scenarios:**

1. **Given** a booking POST with no `reason` field or an empty string, **when** the server processes it, **then** it is refused with HTTP 400 and reason code `REASON_REQUIRED`.
2. **Given** a `reason` longer than 300 characters, **when** the server processes it, **then** it is refused with HTTP 400 and reason code `REASON_TOO_LONG`.
3. **Given** a successful booking with a reason, **when** the booking patient, the assigned doctor, or Staff views the appointment, **then** the reason is included in the response.
4. **Given** patient B, **when** they view any appointment that is not their own, **then** the reason is not disclosed (the whole appointment is already hidden under `NOT_YOUR_APPOINTMENT`).

### User Story 5 — Doctor must write a note to complete a visit (Priority: P2)

When a doctor marks a visit completed, they must provide a short free-text note. The note is required, is stored with the appointment, and becomes part of the input to future doctor summaries (Story 3). Completion is refused if the note is missing or blank.

**Why this priority:** P2. Without notes, future doctor summaries have nothing to summarise; but the booking flow itself still works. Behind P1 because no patient is endangered by a missing note today.

**Independent Test:** A tester books a past-dated appointment (dev mode), signs in as the assigned doctor, tries to complete it with no note, sees it refused. They retry with a note, see it accepted. They open the patient and see the note in the past notes list.

**Acceptance Scenarios:**

1. **Given** a doctor marks an appointment completed with no `doctor_note`, **when** the server processes it, **then** it is refused with HTTP 400 and reason code `NOTE_REQUIRED`.
2. **Given** a doctor completes an appointment with a note, **when** anyone with access views that appointment, **then** the note is stored and shown; the patient does NOT see other patients' notes.
3. **Given** a completed visit with a note, **when** the doctor opens that patient later, **then** the note appears in the past notes list and feeds the AI summary.

### User Story 6 — Staff see urgent cases at the top of their view (Priority: P1)

Staff see a dedicated "Urgent cases" section at the top of their view, listing every open urgent case (red flag) in reverse chronological order. Each row shows the patient, reason text, model's category, the model's `why` line, and the time. Staff click **Mark seen** to remove a case from the open list; the case is retained in storage with its `seen_at` timestamp. Red-flagged appointments that *did* get booked also carry an `Urgent` tag everywhere Staff and the booking's doctor see them.

**Why this priority:** P1 alongside the red-flag check itself (Story 1) — a flag with no visible destination is a flag in the void.

**Independent Test:** A tester creates five red-flag bookings as a patient, signs in as Staff, and sees five open urgent cases at the top of the view with the right categories. They click Mark seen on one, see it leave the open list. They sign in as the doctor on one of the booked red-flag appointments and see an Urgent tag on it.

**Acceptance Scenarios:**

1. **Given** a red-flag booking was submitted, **when** Staff opens their view, **then** the urgent case is at the top with patient, reason text, category, `why`, and timestamp.
2. **Given** an open urgent case, **when** Staff clicks Mark seen, **then** the case's `seen_at` is set to now and it leaves the open list (but remains in the database for audit).
3. **Given** a red-flag booking that was not refused by the rules, **when** Staff or the booking's assigned doctor views it in a schedule, **then** the appointment carries a visible `Urgent` tag.
4. **Given** a red-flag booking that *was* refused by the rules (e.g. surgery session), **when** Staff opens their view, **then** the urgent case still appears (it was flagged, even if no appointment exists).

### Edge Cases

- **Reason in a mix of languages.** The red-flag prompt is written to handle English, Hindi, or Hinglish phrasings. S31–S35 in `reason_samples.csv` cover alternate wordings.
- **Negation.** "No sudden loss of vision" and similar must not trigger. The prompt covers this explicitly.
- **Very long reason.** `reason` is capped at 300 characters by the backend; red-flag prompts use the full text up to that cap.
- **PHI boundaries.** All profile and reason data for the red-flag and summary calls stays in the *volatile* suffix of the prompt (never the cacheable prefix). See conventions.md §10.
- **API key absent.** Fact of the Day and summary fall back cleanly; the red-flag check fails closed (booking refused with `RED_FLAG_CHECK_UNAVAILABLE`). This is exercised by §09's "remove your API key and restart" check.
- **Multiple red flags in one reason.** The model returns one category (the most severe or most specific). The case is still a single urgent record.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

#### Data (adds to Iteration 1)

- **FR-001:** The system MUST load `data/data-pack-02/past_visits.csv` as `completed` appointments (with `reason` and `doctor_note`) on startup, alongside the Iteration 1 loader. These rows count for the follow-up rule in Iteration 1.
- **FR-002:** The system MUST load `data/data-pack-02/patient_profiles.csv` into a new `PatientProfile` structure (one per patient), holding `year_of_birth`, `general_health`, and `allergies`.
- **FR-003:** The `Appointment` entity MUST gain two new fields: `reason` (required on create, ≤300 chars) and `doctor_note` (required on complete).

#### Appointment reason (R3)

- **FR-004:** Every `POST /appointments` MUST include a `reason` of 1–300 characters. Missing → `REASON_REQUIRED`. Too long → `REASON_TOO_LONG`.
- **FR-005:** The `reason` MUST be visible to the booking patient, the assigned doctor, and Staff. It MUST NOT be visible to any other patient.

#### Doctor note (R2)

- **FR-006:** `POST /appointments/{id}/complete` MUST require a non-empty `doctor_note` field. Missing → `NOTE_REQUIRED`.
- **FR-007:** Completed `doctor_note` entries MUST be included in the summary input (FR-014) and in the patient's past-notes view for the assigned doctor.

#### LLM module (R4, R5, R6)

- **FR-008:** All LLM code MUST live in a single `backend/app/llm/` package. One chain per feature: `fact_of_the_day`, `check_red_flags`, `doctor_summary`. Each chain is a LangChain `prompt | model | parser` composition.
- **FR-009:** The LLM provider and model MUST be configurable via `.env` (`LLM_PROVIDER`, `LLM_MODEL`, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`). Default: Anthropic Claude Haiku 4.5 for red-flag and Fact of the Day; Sonnet 4.6 for summary.
- **FR-010:** Each chain MUST have a timeout. Default: 5s for red-flag, 8s for Fact and summary. Timeouts are configurable via `.env`.
- **FR-011:** Red-flag output MUST be parsed via Pydantic (`PydanticOutputParser` or `with_structured_output`) into `{ red_flag: bool, category: str | None, why: str }`, where `category` is one of `sudden_vision_loss`, `curtain_or_shadow`, `floaters_or_flashes`, `chemical_or_injury`, `severe_pain_headache_haloes`, or `null` when `red_flag` is false.
- **FR-012:** The red-flag prompt MUST explain each of the five categories with positive and negative examples (brief §07) and MUST instruct the model to flag when unsure.

#### Red-flag flow (R4)

- **FR-013:** `POST /appointments` MUST call `check_red_flags(reason)` *before* `validate_booking`. If `red_flag=true`, the response MUST include `red_flag: { category, why, emergency_message }` in addition to the booking outcome. The `emergency_message` is the exact literal text in the handout §04-R4.
- **FR-014:** A positive red-flag classification MUST create an `UrgentCase` record (patient, reason text, category, why, created_at, appointment_id nullable), persisted regardless of whether the booking subsequently succeeds or fails.
- **FR-015:** If `check_red_flags` raises (timeout, API error, parser failure), the system MUST refuse the booking with HTTP 503 and reason code `RED_FLAG_CHECK_UNAVAILABLE`. No `Appointment` row and no `UrgentCase` row are created.

#### Fact of the Day (R5)

- **FR-016:** `GET /fact-of-the-day` MUST return `{ fact: str, caveat: "General information, not medical advice" }`. The caveat MUST be set by backend code, never by the LLM.
- **FR-017:** The endpoint MUST accept an optional `previous` query param; when supplied, the prompt MUST instruct the model to return a *different* fact.
- **FR-018:** The prompt MUST forbid naming a condition, treatment, medicine, or product name. The response MUST be ≤2 sentences.
- **FR-019:** If the LLM call fails, the endpoint MUST return a fixed fallback fact (`"Looking at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds every 20 minutes helps rest tired eyes."`) with the caveat; HTTP 200.

#### Doctor summary (R6)

- **FR-020:** `GET /doctor/patients/{patient_id}/summary` MUST be callable only by a signed-in doctor. Any other role → HTTP 403 `NOT_YOUR_APPOINTMENT`.
- **FR-021:** The summary prompt input MUST include: the patient's profile (age derived from `year_of_birth`, `general_health`, `allergies`), today's booked appointment reason (if any, scoped to the requesting doctor), and every completed visit from every doctor with `(date, doctor_name, speciality, doctor_note)`, newest first.
- **FR-022:** The summary prompt MUST instruct the LLM to restate only what is recorded, name the doctor who recorded each item, and never add diagnoses, risks, warnings, or advice of its own.
- **FR-023:** The response MUST include `{ summary: str, past_visits: [...] }`. The UI label "AI summary of recorded notes. Check the notes below." MUST be rendered client-side; the full past-notes list MUST always be shown, even when `summary` is a fallback.
- **FR-024:** If the summary call fails, `summary` MUST be set to `"Summary unavailable. See the notes below."` and `past_visits` MUST still be populated. HTTP 200.

#### Urgent cases (R4 UI)

- **FR-025:** `GET /urgent-cases` MUST return all `UrgentCase` rows where `seen_at IS NULL`, newest first. Staff-only.
- **FR-026:** `POST /urgent-cases/{id}/seen` MUST set `seen_at = now` on the case. Idempotent. Staff-only.
- **FR-027:** Appointments linked to an UrgentCase MUST be marked with an `urgent: true` flag in Staff and doctor views.

#### Context engineering (conventions §10)

- **FR-028:** Each chain's prompt MUST be split into a **cacheable stable prefix** (system prompt, category dictionary, format instructions, few-shot examples) and a **volatile suffix** (the single current input: reason text, previous fact, or patient record). No PHI in the prefix, ever.
- **FR-029:** Each chain MUST log the input token count on every call. Red-flag calls MUST stay ≤2,000 input tokens; summary ≤4,000; Fact of the Day ≤3,000.
- **FR-030:** A chain that would exceed its budget MUST fail-closed as if the LLM call failed (FR-015 for red-flag, FR-019 for Fact, FR-024 for summary) rather than silently truncating patient context.

#### Non-regression

- **FR-031:** Every Iteration 1 clinic rule and every Iteration 1 acceptance scenario MUST continue to pass unchanged. `rules.py` and `slots.py` MUST NOT be modified in this feature (CODEOWNERS enforces).

### Key Entities (changes from Iteration 1)

- **Appointment** — gains `reason: str (1..300)` on create and `doctor_note: str` on completion. Both required; both scoped by role on reads.
- **PatientProfile** (new) — `patient_id` (fk, PK), `year_of_birth: int`, `general_health: str`, `allergies: str`.
- **UrgentCase** (new) — `id`, `patient_id` (fk), `appointment_id` (fk, nullable), `reason_text`, `category`, `why`, `created_at`, `seen_at` (nullable).

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001:** 100% of the five sample red-flag reasons in handout §09 trigger the emergency response and create an urgent case with the correct category.
- **SC-002:** 0 Fact-of-the-Day outputs, across 20 refresh clicks, name a specific condition, treatment, medicine, or product. (Brief §05 target: "0 answers that name a condition or suggest treatment".)
- **SC-003:** 0 doctor summaries, across the three §09 patients (P03, P15, P20), include a diagnosis/risk/advice not present in the recorded notes.
- **SC-004:** Every Iteration 1 acceptance scenario (13 rows + the stretch 38-row harness) continues to pass.
- **SC-005:** With the API key removed, Fact of the Day shows its fallback, the summary shows its fallback, and booking is refused with `RED_FLAG_CHECK_UNAVAILABLE`. No appointment is silently booked.
- **SC-006:** The red-flag check's measured input-token p95 is ≤2,000 tokens across the 35 rows in `reason_samples.csv`.
- **SC-007:** On stable prompts, the Anthropic prompt-cache hit rate for the red-flag chain reaches ≥70% within a session of 20 calls (conventions §10).
- **SC-008:** No cross-patient disclosure of `reason` or `doctor_note` (patient B cannot read patient A's reason or note by any API path).

## Assumptions

- **Provider choice.** Default is Anthropic (Haiku 4.5 for red-flag and Fact; Sonnet 4.6 for summary). Swappable via `.env` per the handout's stretch goal. Rationale is in `research.md`.
- **Emergency text is literal.** The exact string in handout §04-R4 is a safety contract, like the 17 reason codes. It is centralised as a backend constant and never templated through the LLM.
- **Classroom-grade auth stays.** The classroom bearer-token scheme from Iteration 1 is sufficient for the doctor-only summary endpoint. The next real-auth upgrade is Iteration 6 (MCP).
- **Urgent cases persist across restarts.** They are a safety record; container restarts must not lose them. The Postgres table covers this.
- **Fact of the Day is not personalised in Iter 2.** The brief mentions saving per-patient facts only as a stretch goal.
- **No RAG, no agent, no memory.** This iteration is "basic LangChain": prompt | model | parser. Iter 3 adds RAG; Iter 4 adds an agent.
- **Rules engine is frozen.** `rules.py` and `slots.py` are CODEOWNERS-protected and untouched. The red-flag check is a *pre-check*, not a rule.
